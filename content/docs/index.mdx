QRDX is a proof-of-stake blockchain designed for a world with quantum computers. Its validators sign with **ML-DSA-65**, the NIST post-quantum signature standard (FIPS 204, formerly CRYSTALS-Dilithium), and every account can hold a post-quantum key alongside a classic Ethereum-style one.

The chain also has a **native exchange**: order books, concentrated-liquidity pools and perpetual futures are part of the protocol's state, not contracts deployed on top of it. A trade is a transaction the validators execute and every node agrees on.

## How it fits together

| Piece | What it is | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **QRDX node** | The chain: consensus, the EVM, native tokens and the exchange engine. Serves JSON-RPC, REST and WebSocket/SSE streams. | `test.qrdx.org` |
| **QRDX Wallet** | Holds your keys and signs. Browser extension (Chrome, Firefox), web app, and an iPhone app installed from Safari. | [wallet.qrdx.org](https://wallet.qrdx.org) |
| **QRDX Trade** | The trading interface for the native exchange, and a public API over it. | [trade.qrdx.org](https://trade.qrdx.org) |
| **QRDX Explorer** | Blocks, transactions, accounts, tokens and markets. | [explorer.qrdx.org](https://explorer.qrdx.org) |

Every app reads the same node, so they always agree. The wallet signs everything; the apps never hold your keys.

## Why post-quantum

Ethereum and Bitcoin accounts are secured by elliptic-curve signatures. A large enough quantum computer running Shor's algorithm could derive a private key from a public one, and every account whose public key is known (any account that has ever sent a transaction) would be exposed. QRDX's post-quantum accounts are signed with ML-DSA-65, which no known quantum algorithm breaks. [Post-quantum cryptography](/docs/security/post-quantum-cryptography) explains what that protects, and what it does not.

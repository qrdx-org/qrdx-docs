title: Exchange operations
description: Every operation an exchange transaction can carry, its parameters, who may send it, and its gas.
icon: ListOrdered
---

An exchange transaction's `op_type` is the number or the name. Amounts, sizes and prices are decimal strings with at most 18 decimal places. Every operation is all-or-nothing: one that cannot execute is refused whole, changes nothing, and still pays its gas. [Exchange transactions](/docs/build/exchange-transactions) explains the envelope and signing.

**Fee** = gas × `gas_price` (at least 1 gwei = 10⁻⁹ QRDX per gas), paid in QRDX and burned. At the minimum price, 40,000 gas costs 0.00004 QRDX.

<Callout title="Operations 56–83 and the free trading lane">
They arrive with qrdx-node's 2026-10 release, from a fresh genesis. A node without them refuses them as an unknown operation type; `perp_getFeeSchedule` answering is a quick check that a node has them. On such a node the exchange's gas floor is 0.1 gwei, and a **trading operation** (an order, cancel, modify, trigger, TWAP, swap, leverage change, or a batch only of those) sent with `gas_price` 0 pays nothing while the account has free actions: `exchange_getFreeActions(address)` says whether its next one would go free, and why not.
</Callout>

An order's id is the first 16 characters of its transaction hash; the receipt returns it too.

On nodes from the 2026-10 release a market has an **order grid** (`sz_decimals` in `perp_getMarket`): sizes in whole lots of 10^-sz_decimals, and prices of at most 5 significant figures and 6 − sz_decimals decimals (an integer always fits). An order, trigger or modify off the grid is refused. A buy stop and a sell take-profit fire as the mark rises to the trigger; a sell stop and a buy take-profit as it falls. Validator-priced markets are listed by governance (`list_market`); `CREATE_MARKET` lists a bonded deployer's `dex:ASSET` market, with at least 10,000 of `liquidity` for its book.

## Retired

Operations 7–10 (`OPEN_POSITION`, `CLOSE_POSITION`, `PARTIAL_CLOSE`, `ADD_MARGIN`) belonged to an earlier perpetuals engine and are refused; use `PERP_*`. Operation 11, `UPDATE_ORACLE`, sets an oracle price from a configured reporter on development networks only; public networks configure none, and prices come from validators' `ORACLE_VOTE`s.
